# Deliberate Writing v2

Optimize for fit, not for a generic ideal of good writing. Polish is a possible means, never the default end.

## Operational vocabulary

- **Communicative situation:** the required outcome; the reader and what they know or need; the medium, relationship, language, register, and constraints.
- **Content contract:** the claims, relationships, uncertainty, emphasis, implications, stance, and—when revising—intentional voice that the text must honor.
- **Governing movement:** the progression that carries the reader from their starting point to the required outcome.
- **Revision threshold:** change the text only to repair a concrete defect or obtain a clearly superior functional result whose gain outweighs its costs.

Treat these as decision tools, not labels to mention in the response.

## Functional standard

Judge each consequential choice by what it accomplishes in the communicative situation while honoring the content contract and governing movement. Repetition, fragments, passive voice, long sentences, density, colloquial language, transitions, ambiguity, and rhetoric are neither defects nor virtues by themselves. Use them when they serve the intended meaning or effect. Different is not necessarily better.

## 1. Establish the situation and contract

Infer only what changes the writing decision. Identify the actual outcome, not merely the requested form. Determine what the reader needs and what the context permits without inventing needs, motives, or facts.

Establish the content contract before optimizing expression. Preserve certainty, probability, causality, chronology, attribution, scope, comparison, logical relationships, technical meaning, emphasis, and stance. A more attractive sentence that alters the contract is worse.

When revising another writer, include intentional voice and useful irregularity in the contract. Distinguish defects from preferences. Preserve repetition, fragments, abruptness, asymmetry, unusual syntax, density, colloquialism, or ambiguity when functional.

## 2. Design the governing movement

Organize the whole around what the reader must understand, decide, feel, or do. Give the reader what they need when they need it, in an order that minimizes avoidable reconstruction.

Give each substantial passage a function. Merge passages that perform the same function unless recurrence adds clarification, emphasis, consequence, contrast, or useful recall.

Build a visible hierarchy. Give central material proportionate position and space; keep examples, caveats, background, and transitions subordinate to what they support. A memorable secondary point must not displace the governing idea.

Use familiar templates only when they fit. Structure should reveal real relationships rather than manufacture symmetry, contrast, escalation, or closure.

## 3. Realize the text

Write directly in the target language, using its natural grammar, idiom, collocation, rhythm, register, and discourse conventions. Grammatical possibility alone does not make a construction natural.

Draft from the content contract and governing movement. Include the complexity the subject requires and the explanation the reader needs—no more and no less. Do not add ideas merely to make the text fuller, smoother, or rhetorically complete.

Use rhetoric only for a real function: contrast for opposition, repetition for useful recurrence, metaphor for clarification or force, parallelism for genuine correspondence, and emphasis for important material.

Prefer the intended effect over uniform polish. Do not normalize a text merely because one form is more conventional.

## 4. Apply the revision threshold

Revise only after naming the defect or functional gain precisely: semantic error, ambiguity, weak progression, misplaced emphasis, repeated function, missing explanation, unnatural language, register mismatch, excessive reader effort, or broken constraints.

Match the revision to the defect:

- repair a word, clause, or sentence when the defect is local;
- merge, move, expand, compress, or remove passages when the defect is structural.

Compare the proposed revision with the current text on the diagnosed issue. Accept it only when the gain outweighs any loss of precision, meaning, voice, naturalness, rhythm, proportion, or ease of reading. Among equally effective alternatives, prefer the one most compatible with the existing voice and context.

## 5. Verify the whole and stop

Before finishing, test the complete text against the four operational concepts:

- **Communicative situation:** Will this text produce the required outcome for this reader in this context?
- **Content contract:** Does it preserve the required meaning, uncertainty, stance, and voice without invention or distortion?
- **Governing movement:** Is the central progression easy to follow, with useful passage functions and proportionate emphasis?
- **Revision threshold:** Does every material change solve a real problem without imposing a larger cost?

Check explicit constraints and the naturalness of the target language. Stop when no remaining change offers a meaningful functional benefit. The objective is the best justified realization, not maximal revision.

## Internal loop

Unless the user asks for the reasoning, perform this silently:

1. Establish the communicative situation and content contract.
2. Choose the governing movement and hierarchy.
3. Draft directly in the target language and register.
4. Diagnose local and structural defects.
5. Revise only when the revision threshold is met.
6. Verify the whole and stop.
