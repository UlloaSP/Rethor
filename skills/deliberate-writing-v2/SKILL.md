# Deliberate Writing v2

Optimize for fit, not for a generic ideal of good writing.

The right wording depends on what the text must accomplish, what it must mean, who will read it, and where it will appear. Elegance, brevity, formality, simplicity, vividness, and polish are possible means, never default ends.

## Functional standard

Judge each choice by its function in this text.

Repetition, fragments, passive voice, long sentences, technical density, colloquial language, explicit transitions, ambiguity, and rhetorical devices can all be appropriate. Preserve or introduce them when they serve the intended meaning or effect.

For a consequential choice, ask:

- What does it accomplish here?
- Does the context require that function?
- Is it natural in the target language and register?
- Would another realization serve the same meaning better?

A different formulation is not necessarily a better one.

## Establish the task

Before drafting, infer only what matters to the writing decision:

- the purpose and required outcome;
- the intended reader and their likely knowledge;
- what the reader should understand, decide, feel, or do;
- the language, register, medium, and social relationship;
- the required precision and explicit constraints.

Then establish the **content contract**: the claims, distinctions, relationships, uncertainty, emphasis, implications, and stance that the text must preserve.

Style must not alter certainty, probability, causality, chronology, attribution, scope, comparison, or technical meaning. A more attractive sentence that changes the content contract is worse.

## Design the whole

Identify the text’s **governing movement**: the explanation, argument, instruction, request, narrative, decision, or other progression that should organize it.

Choose an order that gives the reader what they need when they need it. Make important relationships explicit when inference would create ambiguity or avoidable effort; leave them implicit when the context already carries them.

Give each substantial passage a function. It should state, explain, support, qualify, exemplify, contrast, connect, redirect, or conclude. When two passages perform the same function, merge them unless the recurrence adds clarification, emphasis, consequence, contrast, or useful recall.

Build a visible hierarchy. Give central ideas proportionate position and space; keep examples, caveats, background, and transitions subordinate to what they support. A memorable secondary point must not displace the governing idea.

Use familiar templates only when they fit the content. Structure should reveal the intended relationships rather than manufacture symmetry, contrast, escalation, or closure.

## Realize the text

Write directly in the target language, using its natural grammar, idiom, collocation, rhythm, register, and discourse conventions. Grammatical possibility alone does not make a construction natural.

Draft from the content contract and governing movement. Include the complexity the subject requires and the explanation the reader needs—no more and no less.

Use rhetoric as a tool:

- contrast for a real opposition;
- repetition for a useful recurrence;
- metaphor for clarification, characterization, or force;
- parallelism for elements that genuinely correspond;
- emphasis for material that deserves prominence.

Prefer the intended effect over uniform polish. Variation, abruptness, asymmetry, technical density, or deliberate ambiguity may be the correct realization.

## Diagnose and revise

Revise only against a concrete defect or a clearly superior functional realization. Name the problem precisely: semantic error, ambiguity, weak progression, misplaced emphasis, repeated function, missing explanation, unnatural language, register mismatch, syntactic awkwardness, or another observable failure.

Match the revision to the defect:

- fix a word, clause, or sentence when the problem is local;
- merge, move, expand, compress, or remove passages when the problem is structural.

Compare the revision with the current version on the diagnosed issue. Accept it only when the gain outweighs any loss of precision, meaning, voice, naturalness, rhythm, proportion, or ease of reading.

When revising another writer, distinguish defects from preferences. Preserve intentional voice and useful irregularity. Among equally effective alternatives, choose the one most compatible with the surrounding text.

## Finish at the level of the whole

Before finishing, inspect the complete text:

- Is the governing movement easy to follow?
- Does every substantial passage have a useful function?
- Does recurrence develop meaning rather than merely restate it?
- Does emphasis reflect communicative importance?
- Does the reader perform avoidable reconstruction?
- Does the ending fulfill the actual purpose?
- Does the final text honor the content contract and voice?

Local quality does not guarantee global quality. A good sentence may still need to move, merge, or disappear when it weakens the whole.

Stop when no remaining change offers a meaningful functional benefit. The objective is the best justified realization, not maximal revision.

## Internal loop

Unless the user asks for the reasoning, perform this silently:

1. Infer the communicative situation.
2. Establish the content contract.
3. Identify the governing movement and hierarchy.
4. Draft in the target language and register.
5. Diagnose and repair local and structural defects.
6. Verify the whole against purpose, meaning, reader, and voice.
7. Stop when further revision lacks a clear benefit.
